The EU AI act is a major **law governing AI usage and regulations**.
# Field of Application
The act is aimed at *providers* and *deployers* of AI systems.
- **Providers are developers of AI systems that place it on the market**. They can be natural or legal persons, authorities, or other bodies. They must fulfil strict requirements in terms of quality, safety, and ethical standards.
- **Deployers are user of an AI system under their own responsibility**. Their own responsibility depends on the risk of the AI system used. Like providers, they can be legal or natural persons, authorities, or other bodies.
# Risks
The EU AI Act categorizes AI systems in four risk categories:
- **Unacceptable**: these systems are prohibited, as they pose serious threats to fundamental rights. The act lists such systems, including social rating systems or manipulative AI.
- **High-risk**: the main focus of the act, these are AI applied to healthcare, the workplace, or law enforcement. They have strict requirements, such as having a risk management system, documentation, and a duty of information for the provider. These requirements are checked as part of the *conformity assessment procedure*.
- **Limited risk**: these are systems whose risk can be minimized by simple transparency. For example, labelling artificially-generated media, and obligation to disclose AI interactions to end users.
- **Minimal risk**: these can be used freely, and include, for example, a spam filter. You can still comply with the act on a voluntary basis with a code of conduct.
